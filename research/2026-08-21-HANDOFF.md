The theory was: it failed on the full grid because typed-lab data was too thin

### 1.3 Neural / EINN / GNN / 52-state spatial NN — REJECTED

No neural net has topped FluSight hospital-admissions seasons. Winners that
look like "ML" are LightGBM quantile regression (UMass-flusion, 2023-24) or

A 52-node GNN cannot learn a 52×52 graph from ~78 epidemic snapshots. If the
graph is fixed and you learn a coupling, that is `iota`, which must be **fixed
a priori**. A 52-channel multivariate net is three independent seasons of
length ~26. Untrainable.

### 1.4 52-region BNGL metapopulation with movement — REJECTED

The **signal** is real (see §2). The container is not. Interstate travel is a
rounding error on within-state mixing. The 1-week lead is epidemiological
asynchrony, not commuting. A coupled 260-species PF dies in dimension, loses
per-state parallelism, and adds unidentified movement rates. Extra fitted
dimensions are how the current PF got too wide.

### 1.5 Global interval-width multiplier — REJECTED as a ship item

`q'(τ) = m + s·(q(τ)−m)`, one `s` per horizon, leave-one-season-out:
**+0.3% pooled relWIS** against a ~5% noise floor. In-sample looks like
+1–5%. 2023-24 and 2024-25 want `s≈0.7–0.9`; 2025-26 wants wider and loses
5%. The house rule about fitted quantities, reproduced on a single scalar.

A first run of this reported a fake +25% because the upper-quantile array was
reversed. `research/wis-decomposition/width_sweep.py` now asserts agreement
with `flubnf.wis.wis` at `s=1.0` (max rel. diff 4.5e-16) before reporting.
Any future rescoring script **must carry the same assertion**. A scoring bug
that makes results look better is the one nobody double-checks.

Detail: `research/wis-decomposition/FINDINGS.md`

### 1.6 Second Fourier harmonic (`eps2`, `phi2`) — already closed in-tree

`flubnf/templates/SIHRS_pop_min.bngl` header: the semi-annual harmonic made
`beta` two-humped and did **not** produce two epidemic peaks (susceptible

## 2. The defect you are actually aiming at

Production PF WIS, 16,775 cells, `app/state/retro_seal`:

| component | share of WIS |
| --- | --- |
| median \|y − m\| | **6.4%** |
| dispersion (interval widths) | **49.9%** |
| penalty (truth outside interval) | **43.7%** |

50% intervals cover 55–60% (too wide). Swapping PF median for analogue median
was previously measured at **+0.003 relWIS**; swapping spread at **+0.070**.
The open problem is turns (Jan 2025 peak, Feb 2024 plateau). CDC's own
ensemble had **6% coverage** at the 2-week horizon on 2025-01-04.

Catching a turn does not show up in the 6.4%. It shows up in the **44%
penalty**. Mean-model improvements that reduce false turns are therefore in
scope for WIS even though the median is already fine. Mean-model improvements
on ordinary weeks are not.

~5% pooled relWIS is the noise floor. Pre-register gates against **turn-week
relWIS** first, pooled second. A gain confined to h=1 is a nowcast result;
report it as such and do not seat on it.

Equal-weight quantile averaging: ensemble interval width at any level is the
**arithmetic mean** of member widths. A candidate wider than the current mean
arithmetically guarantees a wider ensemble. Measure candidate interval width
against incumbents **before** computing WIS. That is a free pre-screen.

## 3. Build 1 (highest value) — national-growth term in the PF

### Why

Leave-one-out national log-growth of admissions at week *t* predicts a
state's own growth at *t+1* after AR(1) **and** a Fourier seasonal (the thing
`eps1`/`phi1` already give the PF):

| held-out | ΔR² | RMSE reduction | turn-week RMSE reduction |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2023-24 | +0.081 | +5.1% | +8.9% |
| 2024-25 | +0.032 | +2.1% | +2.4% |
| 2025-26 | +0.118 | +7.9% | **+14.7%** |

Partial correlation **+0.47**. This is **this year's realised wave**, not the
calendar. The analogue pools *prior* seasons, calendar-matched. The PF sees
this season, one state. Nobody currently sees "the Midwest peaked last week."
That is the missing information set, and it is vintage-true (target stream
only).

Reproduce: `./.venv/bin/python research/spatial-nowcast-probe/probe.py`
Writeup: `research/spatial-nowcast-probe/FINDINGS.md`

### What to implement

Keep per-state filters, 10k particles, 5 fitted parameters, embarrassingly
parallel. Do **not** couple the ODEs.

Specify on **growth, not level**. A level-form importation
`sum w_ss' A_s'/N_s'` restates prevalence (the occupancy-ratio trap).

Recommended form, `iota` **not fitted**:

```
beta_s(t)  *=  exp( iota * (g_nat^{-s}(t) - g_s^{obs}(t)) )
```

or the additive equivalent in the growth rate. `g_nat^{-s}(t)` is the
population-weighted log-growth of vintage-true admissions across all other
FluSight jurisdictions as of the same as-of. `g_s^{obs}(t)` is that state's
own most recent vintage-true log-growth. Both are computed **outside** the
BNGL, at materialize time, and passed as tokens or as a short lookup table
of weekly values (same pattern as `{{POP}}`, `{{S0FRAC}}`).

**Fix `iota` before any PF run.** Suggested starting value: the LOSO
regression coefficient of `g_{t+1}` on `g_nat` given `g_t` and Fourier
seasonal, averaged across the two non-scored seasons, then **shrunk toward
zero** (e.g. multiply by 0.5) so the first arm is conservative. Put the
number in the spec / a constant, not in a `*_var` line.

Alternative, if BNGL injection of a weekly series is painful: a
**forecast-time shrink** of each particle's implied 1–4 week growth toward
last observed `g_nat`, same fixed `iota`. That is less mechanistic and easier
to A/B. If you do both, pre-register which one is primary.

HHS-region leave-one-out growth was slightly stronger in-sample than
national. Start with **national** (one series, no region table, no NY/NJ
edge cases). A region arm is a sensitivity, not the gate.

### What not to do in this build

- Do not fit `iota`.
- Do not add a binomial/Gaussian observation channel on national growth
  (extra ESS, extra width).
- Do not build a mobility matrix.
- Do not change particle count, jitter, or the other five parameters.

### Evaluation (pre-register before the first fit)

- Arms: P0 production PF; P1 PF + fixed-iota national growth. Same seeds,
  same vintages, paired cells.
- Then the **ensemble** comparison that actually ships: E0 = 50/50
  (P0, analogue); E1 = 50/50 (P1, analogue). Identical analogue.
- Selection / iota choice: 2023-24 + 2024-25 pooled only, or freeze iota
  from the probe regression **now** and do not retune after seeing scores.
- Gate 1 (turn): E1 beats E0 on Feb-2024 and Jan-2025 cells, paired.
- Gate 2 (seat): E1 beats E0 on the full grid, all three seasons.
- Floor: P1 member relWIS < 1.1 in every season.
- Kill: turn-week relWIS movement < 5%, or P1 interval width > P0 at the
  same coverage.
- Report by horizon. A gain only at h=1 is not a seat.

Vintage: `g_nat` must be computed from `vintage_path(asof)`, never from the
latest file. `app/core/data.py` already fails loud on a missing vintage.

### Where it plugs in

- Token / series construction: next to `materialize_model` /
  `resolve_state` in `flubnf/sihrs_fit.py`, or a tiny helper under `flubnf/`
  that `app/core/engines/pf.py` calls when building each cell.
- BNGL: a **new** template (e.g. `SIHRS_pop_natg.bngl`) copied from
  `SIHRS_pop_min.bngl`, not an in-place edit of production `min`. Production
  stays the control.
- `RunSpec.extra` e.g. `{"variant": "natg", "iota": 0.4}` so the ledger
  records the frozen iota.
- Do not change `analogue.py`. This term is PF-only; the analogue already
  pools states historically.

## 4. Build 2 — last-week completeness (analogue + PF, two different treatments)

### Facts

90 vintages, 7,219 state-weeks. Median report is within 5% of final **at
first issue** (2023-24, 2025-26) or **one week later** (2024-25). 60% of
cells are born settled; 74% by 1 week; 81% by 2 weeks. At lag 2 the median
vintage/final is 1.000 in every season.

The moving piece is week T. Lag-0 first/final:

| season | median | p10 | share < 0.95 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2023-24 | **1.000** | 0.93 | 12% |
| 2024-25 | 0.966 / 0.918* | 0.71 | 44–63% |
| 2025-26 | 0.982 / 0.929* | 0.75 | 41–56% |

\*first table is all first-issues; second is the as-of==week (true last
point) slice. Mean |log-bias| of the last point is 0.12.

**2023-24 was a different reporting regime.** LOSO state-median completeness
applied to lag-0: 2024-25 **+7.2%** |log| reduction, 2025-26 **+9.3%**,
2023-24 **−99%** (doubles the error). You cannot fit `c` across that break.

An under-reported last point looks like a **decelerating epidemic**. That is
the premature-turn mechanism. Completeness and the national-growth term
(§3) are complementary at turns: one fixes the own-state last point, the
other says the country has not turned.

Reproduce settling: `./.venv/bin/python research/spatial-nowcast-probe/settle.py`

Hub precedent: `Cornell_JHU-hierarchSIR` already ships a reporting model
(Dirichlet-multinomial as a sequential beta-binomial; completeness from a
four-week rolling window of past **revisions**). PSI-PROF substitutes a
statistical baseline when there is no epi signal. Do not invent a third
scheme if Cornell's rolling-window-of-revisions is enough.

### What to implement — split by member, they are not the same input

**Analogue** (`app/core/engines/analogue.py`, `flubnf/analogue.py`) **is**
`last_point × historical_ratio`. A 7% hole in T is a 7% hole at every
horizon. This member wants a nowcasted **anchor**.

- `c_s` = state's median first/final at lag 0, estimated **only on 2024-25
  and 2025-26**, never 2023-24. Shrink toward the national median. Freeze
  before scoring.
- `anchor' = anchor / c_s` (first-issue is typically below final, so this
  scales up).
- Widen analogue quantiles by the residual of `c` (historical |log|
  residual, ~0.12 pooled) so you do not ship false precision.
- Optional sensitivity: blend T with T−1 (T−1 is the last nearly-settled
  point). Primary arm is scaled-T.

**PF:** keep the observed `y`. Change the observation model for the newest
`L ∈ {0,1}` weeks:

```
y_t^(as-of, lag L)  ~  NegBin( c_{s,L} * mu_t,  r_L )
```

with `r_L ≥ r` at lag 0 (more dispersion, not less). `c_{s,L}` is computed
offline from Delphi/hub vintages, same freeze as analogue, **2023-24
excluded from the fit of c**. Weeks older than 2 are already settled; leave
them alone.

`RunSpec.weeks_to_drop` / `weeks_to_nowcast` already exist;
`weeks_to_nowcast` is a documented no-op. **Do not** implement the no-op as
"write a nowcasted number into the `.exp` and treat it as data." That is
false precision and it is how a PF becomes more sure of a number you made
up. `weeks_to_drop = 1` is the blunt fallback (ignore last point entirely)
if `c` looks stale in production; it is dumber than a good `c` (throws away
the last point's **direction**) and safer than a bad `c`.

### Evaluation

- Do **not** claim a 2023-24 retrospective for this candidate. Pre-register
  that in the commit message / README.
- Fit `c` on 2024-25, evaluate on 2025-26, and the reverse. Those two
  directions already agreed on sign in the probe.
- Gate on turn cells in those two seasons, then pooled.
- Expected pooled relWIS movement is small (null is the most probable
  outcome on the full grid). The reason to build is turn-week penalty, and
  analogue-median bias that currently lands on all four horizons.
- Combine with Build 1 as E2 = 50/50 (P1 + completeness PF, analogue with
  scaled anchor) only **after** each is gated alone. Do not confound.

### Vintage

Completeness **must** be estimated from the vintage archive
(`flubnf.settings.ARCHIVE` / `app.core.data.vintages`), first-issue vs
latest, as `settle.py` does. Never from a single final file.

## 7. Build 5 — flexible `beta(t)`, only if 1–4 disappoint on turns

PSI-PROF (most consistent mechanistic name on FluSight; 2024–25 top
*individual* model) uses **two or more arctangents** for `beta(t)`, MCMC,
then **stochastic simulations** from the posterior — not ODE + NegBin
around a single path. Cornell-hierarchSIR uses a spline with **across-season
hierarchical** priors. That inductive bias is piecewise rises and flats, which
can represent a plateau. A second Fourier term cannot, and was already tried.

If you build this:

- New template, not an edit of `SIHRS_pop_min.bngl`.
- Two changepoints max, or a spline whose coefficients shrink to
  `historical_priors` (already in-tree). Count fitted dimensions. If Reff/
  `mult` posterior **widens**, it is a net loss even if the plateau median
  improves. Measure interval width before WIS (the free pre-screen).

  has them. Parsimony is the current PF's load-bearing constraint.

## 9. Methodological rules — non-negotiable

1. **Echo test = incremental R² over AR(1), not correlation with the target.**
   Adult/admissions growth is autocorrelated (~0.44). Raw lag-0 correlation
   is not an echo verdict. The member-search memo's §3 triage passed two
   rejected candidates because it screened association, not residual
   information.

2. **Vintage-true or it does not count.** A 2026 re-analysis (Bracher & Funk)
   of a *Nature* AI-vs-CDC claim: 11% WIS "win" was **1.00 on unrevised
   state-weeks**. Final-data numbers are upper bounds. Age-pilot numbers in
   `candidate-age2/` are final-data and labelled as such.

3. **External APIs fail loudly.** Delphi `fluview_clinical` takes a
   comma-separated region list (3 requests, not 624). A retry loop that
   swallows HTTP 429 reports rate-limiting as "no data." Bare `except` in
   classifiers has already turned RecursionError into a clean-looking "thin
   data" table.

4. **Scoring scripts assert against `flubnf.wis.wis`.** See §1.5.

5. **Do not evaluate completeness on 2023-24.** Regime break is measured.

6. **Law 1 still holds: do not fit `iota`, `theta`, coupling strengths, or
   ensemble weights on past-season scores.**

7. **Law 2 still holds: no panel gates.** Full 50-state grid, or the result
   does not transfer.
