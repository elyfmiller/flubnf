# Forecasting another target with FluBNF

FluBNF is built for one target: weekly influenza hospital admissions
(`wk inc flu hosp`) for the 53 FluSight locations, read from a clone of
the FluSight hub. Its own-data path and its Sandbox reach further with no
code. This page is for outside groups, and for students starting another
target. For each kind of change it says what works today, what the
console assumes, and what needs code. "Not supported yet" means nothing
in the console does it today.

| Change | Works today without code | What the console assumes | Needs code |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Your own weekly data** | Upload a CSV on the Forecast or Retrospective tab's **Your data**, or the Data tab's **Your datasets**: a date, a group and a value on each row, and a population if you have one. A file of one series needs no group column. The upload box's **Template CSV** is a file that works. The check stores nothing, and names each problem with its rows. The Forecast tab forecasts from the data's newest week or an earlier one. The Retrospective tab replays it week by week, on the data as each week saw it when the file has an as_of column or comes as a folder of snapshot files, one per as_of. The Groundhog forecasts any weekly count or rate. The Liu-West filter also fits counts that have a population, once the engine is installed. The Output tab's **Your data** keeps the forecasts as hubverse CSVs named FluBNF-Groundhog and FluBNF-SIHRS-PF, never submitted. They are scored against an in-house persistence baseline. `flubnf dataset validate` and `flubnf dataset import` read the same files. | One row per group and week. Every date in a file falls on one weekday. A date that is not a Saturday moves to the Saturday that ends its MMWR week (Sunday to Saturday), with a notice: under a plain `date` header, a Sunday moves 6 days on. Under a header that names the week's end (`week_ending`, `target_end_date`), a Sunday, Monday or Tuesday is refused: most of such a week lies in the MMWR week before. Daily rows are refused. At most 200 groups, 1,500,000 rows and 100 MB in one upload. Snapshot files must all hold the same groups. Case, spaces and underscores in headers do not matter, common names are read, and the check asks which column is which when a header is unknown. A group name is at most 40 letters (any script), digits, spaces and underscores: "Baden Württemberg" is read, "Baden-Württemberg" is refused. The Groundhog needs 30 earlier weeks like the forecast week: weeks of strictly earlier seasons whose MMWR week is within two of its own, over all groups. One series needs about six earlier seasons, three groups about two; the check says when the newest week has too few. | Forecasting or replaying a dataset from a terminal: not supported yet (`flubnf dataset` checks, imports, lists and deletes). The upload limits: `app/core/datasets/report.py` `Limits`. The header names: `app/core/datasets/parsing.py` `ROLE_ALIASES`. The Groundhog's 30: `flubnf/analogue.py` `MIN_DONORS`, which the FluSight forecasts use too. |
| **Another target** (deaths, cases, ED visits, a rate) | Any weekly count or rate, as your own data (the row above). The FluSight hub's second target, `target-data/target-ed-visits-prop.csv` in a hub clone, reads as a rate dataset, which the Groundhog forecasts. | Horizons 0 to 3 weeks and FluSight's 23 quantile levels, for every target (locked). Count forecasts are whole numbers; rates keep their decimals. The hub path forecasts one quantity, `wk inc flu hosp`, and scores it against FluSight-baseline. | The horizons and levels: `app.core.horizons:HORIZONS` and `app.core.submit:QUANTILES` (locked; the stored forecasts are built on them). The hub's target and names: `app/core/submit.py` (`TEAM_ABBR`, `MODEL_ABBR`, `RATE_CHANGE_TARGET`), `app/core/data.py` `LIVE_TARGET` and `app/core/relwis.py` `BASELINE`; `wk inc flu hosp` and `FluSight-baseline` are written out in other modules too. Replaying the hub's ED-visit archive as each week saw it: not supported yet, since its snapshot files do not all hold the same groups. |
| **Another pathogen** (RSV, COVID-19, ...) | The Groundhog on your own data: it fits nothing, and its donors are your data's own earlier weeks, unless you add FluSurv-NET's influenza donors on the form. In the Sandbox, write a model of your own (the SEIHR template marks every pathogen and population value `# EDIT`) and fit it with the Liu-West filter to one series at a time: a hub jurisdiction, one group of a stored dataset, or a CSV ([SANDBOX.md](SANDBOX.md)). | On your own data, the Liu-West filter fits the shipped influenza SIHRS: its fixed values (a 3.2-day generation time, 85% susceptible at the season start, transmission that peaks once a year) are influenza's. For another pathogen, write a model in the Sandbox. The Groundhog's two-week donor window and its 30 donors are the shipped FluSight values. The Oracle step runs on FluSight hub data only. | A Sandbox model on a dataset's Forecast or Replay: not supported yet; those forms offer the shipped SIHRS and the Groundhog. Sandbox experiments replay FluSight hub seasons only. The SIHRS's fixed values: `flubnf/templates/SIHRS_pop_min.bngl` and `flubnf/sihrs_priors.py` (the template is locked as `pf.template`). |
| **Another geography** | Your own data, with your own groups: up to 200 in one upload, named in any script. A national row, if the data have one, is scored beside the pooled figure, never inside it. | The hub path forecasts FluSight's 53 locations, keyed by US FIPS codes, from the hub's `auxiliary-data/locations.csv` (the packaged `flubnf/data/locations.csv` when the hub has none). A dataset's national row is found by its name: US, USA, United States, US (national), National, or Overall beside other groups (the console's template's word; alone in its file, Overall is an ordinary group). Any other name, a country's included, is an ordinary group. | The hub's locations: `flubnf/settings.py` `LOCATIONS` and `load_locations`; some modules read the table themselves (`app/core/scoring.py`, `app/core/groundhog.py` and `app/ui/routes/retro.py` among them). The Oracle step's donor bank and the FluSurv-NET bank in `data/banks/` are US data. The national names: `app/core/datasets/checks.py` `NATIONAL_NAMES`. |
| **Another season calendar** (the Southern Hemisphere, a summer peak) | On the Retrospective tab's **Your data**, From and To pick the weeks a replay covers. On the dataset forms, **Season start** under Model settings sets the first week the Liu-West filter fits. | A season runs from August to July (locked). Weeks are MMWR weeks, Sunday to Saturday, with week 53 between weeks 52 and 1. The Groundhog takes its donors only from strictly earlier seasons, by this calendar. A replay's default weeks are October to June of the newest season that holds 8 or more of them; for a Southern Hemisphere season, set From and To. The filter fits from August 1 of the forecast's season unless Season start says otherwise. | `flubnf/analogue.py` `SEASON_BOUNDARY_MONTH` (locked as `season_boundary_month`). The August boundary is also written out in other modules (search for `-08-01` and `>= 8`), among them `app/core/runs.py` `default_season_start` and `app/ui/datasets_ui.py` `replay_window`. The MMWR week: `flubnf/analogue.py` `epiweek`, and the upload's `app/core/datasets/parsing.py` `week_ending`. |

## Trying it with your own data

1. Install FluBNF ([README](../README.md#quick-start)). The Groundhog
   needs no engine. The Liu-West filter needs the engine, which
   `./setup_engine.sh` installs, as the first open of FluBNF does.
2. Open the Forecast tab's **Your data** and upload your CSV, or the
   upload box's **Template CSV** to see one that works. The check reads
   the file, stores nothing, and lists what it read and what it refused.
3. Store it from the check, then forecast a week on the Forecast tab, or
   replay its weeks on the Retrospective tab's **Your data**.

More on own data: the README's
[What it forecasts](../README.md#what-it-forecasts-from-what-data), the
Sandbox's [Your own data](SANDBOX.md#5-your-own-data), and
