Methods

What FluBNF is built on, how the SIHRS model works, and the policies that keep every number verifiable.

Purpose

FluBNF generates real-time probabilistic forecasts of weekly influenza hospital admissions for all 52 US jurisdictions, in the quantile format required by the CDC FluSight forecast hub. Two models run each week: a mechanistic SIHRS model fitted by a sequential particle filter, and a calendar analogue built from historical seasons. Their blend, the ensemble, is the submitted forecast.

The SIHRS model

SIHRS is a compartmental model of influenza transmission. Individuals move from Susceptible to Infected; most infections recover directly, while a small severe fraction is admitted to Hospitalized before reaching Recovered. Immunity wanes, so recovered individuals eventually return to the susceptible pool. The model is written in BNGL, the BioNetGen model-specification language.

Rates. Transmission β(t) varies over the season as a smooth annual harmonic, so the model can produce the winter wave without hand-set breakpoints. Infections resolve at rate γ (fixed from the measured influenza generation time); a fixed severe fraction ρ (the infection-hospitalization ratio) is admitted, and the remaining (1-ρ) recover directly. Hospitalized individuals discharge at γₕ, and immunity wanes at ω, returning people to the susceptible pool.

Why this structure. Three reasons, each tied to the forecasting target. First, hospital admissions are the FluSight target, so H is an explicit compartment: the model fits the actual I to H admission flux, scaled by a fitted reporting ascertainment, rather than a scaled proxy of infections. Second, waning immunity (R back to S) permits repeat seasonal waves, which single-pass SIR-type structures cannot produce. Third, seasonal transmission lets the data determine when transmissibility peaks in each state. Five parameters are fitted per state; every fixed parameter is sourced from published estimates.

Fitting: a sequential particle filter

FluBNF fits the SIHRS model with a particle filter, implemented as fit_type=pf in a fork of PyBNF, Los Alamos National Laboratory's parameter-fitting suite for BioNetGen models. The filter maintains about 10,000 candidate epidemics per state, each a complete SIHRS trajectory with its own parameters. Every week the newest admissions reweight the candidates by how well they explain the data (Liu-West jitter and systematic resampling keep the population diverse), and the surviving spread of trajectories is the forecast uncertainty. This sequential design matches the operational cadence: forecasts update weekly as data arrive, rather than refitting from scratch.

Simulations run on bngsim, an in-process BioNetGen simulation engine. Running simulations in-process removes the per-simulation overhead of launching BNG2.pl, so a full state-season fit completes in seconds. BioNetGen's BNG2.pl performs the one-time network generation for each model.

The ensemble

The submitted forecast blends the particle filter with a calendar analogue: a purely empirical model that, for each forecast week, resamples what happened after historically similar calendar weeks. The two members fail differently; the mechanistic model tracks turning points the analogue cannot anticipate, while the analogue is difficult to beat at short horizons. Blending weights are per-horizon (PF share 0.4 to 0.8 across horizons 1 to 4, with twelve state-specific overrides) and are frozen before the season from held-out years, so nothing is tuned on the season being forecast.

Measured performance

Three full seasons were replayed in real-time conditions: each week the models saw only that week's archived data vintage, submitted, and moved on. Scores are relative weighted interval scores (relWIS) against the official FluSight baseline model; values below 1 beat the baseline.

Data and verification policies

Every run uses vintage data: the data exactly as archived on its forecast date, never revised history. Every run is seeded and reproducible from its ledger entry. Missing weeks are never imputed; they are dropped as rows with calendar-true offsets. All scoring uses the weighted interval score against the official FluSight baseline, the competition's own yardstick, and evaluation touches only data that was available at forecast time.
