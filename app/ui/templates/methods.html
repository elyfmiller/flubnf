Purpose

FluBNF forecasts weekly influenza hospital admissions for all 52 US jurisdictions in the quantile format of the CDC FluSight forecast hub, from two models submitted separately: the Oracle SIHRS, the SIHRS compartment model fitted each week by a particle filter with its forecast growth blended with past seasons' at the same calendar week, and the Groundhog, a calendar analogue with an auxiliary donor bank. Nothing is blended between the two.

The SIHRS compartment model

The SIHRS compartment model, written in BNGL: people move from Susceptible to Infected; most recover directly, a small severe fraction is Hospitalized first, and Recovered immunity wanes back to susceptible.

{{ dg.sihrs(uid='mm') }}

Rates. Transmission β(t) follows a smooth annual harmonic; infections resolve at γ (from the measured generation time), a fixed fraction ρ is hospitalized, discharge is γₕ and immunity wanes at ω.

{{ dg.eq_pf() }}

Why this structure. H is explicit because admissions are the target (the model fits the I to H flux with a fitted ascertainment), waning immunity allows repeat seasonal waves, and seasonal transmission lets each state's data place its peak. Five parameters are fitted per state; ρ, γH and ω are working assumptions, recorded as such in flubnf/sihrs_priors.py.

Model tab: Oracle SIHRS.

Fitting: a sequential particle filter

A particle filter (fit_type=pf in a fork of PyBNF) keeps 10,000 candidate epidemics per state, each a full trajectory with its own parameters. Each week the newest admissions reweight them, a Liu-West kernel jitters the survivors' parameters (scale 0.15), and systematic resampling keeps them diverse; their spread is the forecast uncertainty. Every forecast is a fresh run from August 1 through that week's vintage, so it sees every revision to earlier weeks.

BNG2.pl expands the BNGL model into a reaction network once per state when a run is prepared; the filter then integrates it in process with bngsim, so a state-season fit takes seconds.

The Oracle step: past seasons' growth, blended in

{{ dg.oracle_pipeline(uid='mp') }}

What it does. Each of the filter's forecast sample paths (30,000 per state at three replicates) gets one donor: a growth path from an earlier season within two epiweeks of the forecast week, any jurisdiction, the calendar-donor principle the Groundhog uses. Its growth over each forecast week becomes the geometric mean, half and half, of the filter's and the donor's, propagated in closed form from the filter's own state. One donor per sample, not per forecast, keeps both the filter's uncertainty and the donors' spread.

{{ dg.eq_oracle() }}

Donors. {{ oracle_text.BANK_TEXT.pool }}

Why. A filter carries today's growth forward, which is right while a season keeps its course and wrong at the turns; past seasons at the same calendar week show how seasons usually move on.

How it relates to the Groundhog. The Groundhog applies donor growth ratios to the last observed count, with no mechanism; the Oracle SIHRS applies donor growth to the fitted state, at half weight. The two are submitted as separate models.

Research: the two-strain variant not a shipped model

What it does. Influenza A and B as independent SIHRS circuits, each with its own seasonal transmission and its own susceptible, hospitalized and recovered pools; observed admissions are the ascertained sum, so an early A wave can be followed by a late B wave.

{{ dg.sihrs2(uid='m2') }}

A second observation channel. Besides NHSN admissions, each candidate is weighed by NREVSS typed positives (binomial counts of influenza A among typed specimens), which also set the season-start A/B mix; both channels are vintage data.

{{ dg.eq_pf2s() }}

Why it does not ship. Stronger at turning points but worse on the full grid in the August 2026 evaluation; it stays a research run (the two-strain model page is reachable by URL).

The Groundhog

{{ dg.analogue_ratios(uid='ma') }}

A purely empirical model. For horizon h it pools donor weeks from strictly earlier seasons, any state, within two MMWR epiweeks of the forecast date, and scales the last observed value by the empirical quantiles of their h-week growth ratios; fewer than 30 donors means no forecast. States are pooled because one state gives about five donors per season; the US row is about 2 percent of donors, epiweek 53 sits between 52 and 1, and future values are read by date, never by week label.

The auxiliary donor bank. A second pool built the same way on FluSurv-NET hospitalization rates, a committed, digest-checked file; its ratios are shrunk to the admissions scale (fitted on earlier seasons only) and averaged in at a fixed equal weight. This bank is what separates the Groundhog from the bare calendar analogue; it was pre-registered and chosen over an ILI+ bank.

Coverage. Donors are neither exchangeable nor independent, so no coverage guarantee is claimed; over three replayed seasons the central 50, 80 and 90 percent bands covered 0.51, 0.80 and 0.95.

2021-22 is excluded from the donors: it peaked in April 2022 and the archive starts 2022-02-05, so it survives only as its growth phase and a calendar-matched pool would read March as still growing. The pre-registered exclusion improved all three replayed seasons and 50 of 52 jurisdictions.

Data and verification policies

Every run uses the data exactly as archived on its forecast date, is seeded and reproducible from its ledger entry, and never imputes a missing week; scoring is the weighted interval score against the FluSight baseline.

Two measured caveats (docs/archive/RELEASE-1.0.md): seven of 76 sealed weeks' vintages differ from what was public at the deadline, and sealed scores use unrounded forecasts (a third-decimal difference).

{# the scored-cell rule lives here: this card survives the site harvest #}

A scored cell is one location, week and horizon with positive settled truth, a positive forecast median and a baseline forecast; FluSight keeps cells this drops, so counts can differ.
