Methods

Purpose

FluBNF generates real-time probabilistic forecasts of weekly influenza hospital admissions for all 52 US jurisdictions, in the quantile format required by the CDC FluSight forecast hub. Two models run each week: a mechanistic SIHRS model fitted by a sequential particle filter, and a calendar analogue built from historical seasons. Their blend, the ensemble, is the submitted forecast.

The SIHRS model

SIHRS is a compartmental model of influenza transmission. Individuals move from Susceptible to Infected; most infections recover directly, while a small severe fraction is admitted to Hospitalized before reaching Recovered. Immunity wanes, so recovered individuals eventually return to the susceptible pool. The model is written in BNGL, the BioNetGen model-specification language.

{{ dg.sihrs(uid='mm') }}

Rates. Transmission β(t) varies over the season as a smooth annual harmonic, so the model can produce the winter wave without hand-set breakpoints. Infections resolve at rate γ (fixed from the measured influenza generation time); a fixed severe fraction ρ (the infection-hospitalization ratio) is admitted, and the remaining (1-ρ) recover directly. Hospitalized individuals discharge at γₕ, and immunity wanes at ω, returning people to the susceptible pool.

{{ dg.eq_pf() }}

Why this structure. Three reasons, each tied to the forecasting target. First, hospital admissions are the FluSight target, so H is an explicit compartment: the model fits the actual I to H admission flux, scaled by a fitted reporting ascertainment, rather than a scaled proxy of infections. Second, waning immunity (R back to S) permits repeat seasonal waves, which single-pass SIR-type structures cannot produce. Third, seasonal transmission lets the data determine when transmissibility peaks in each state. Five parameters are fitted per state. Of the fixed values, the recovery rate traces to a cited generation-time study, while ρ, γH and ω are working assumptions with no source behind them; the provenance module (flubnf/sihrs_priors.py) records each value's status, including which ones are assumptions.

Fitting: a sequential particle filter

FluBNF fits the SIHRS model with a particle filter, implemented as fit_type=pf in a fork of PyBNF, Los Alamos National Laboratory's parameter-fitting suite for BioNetGen models. The filter maintains 10,000 candidate epidemics per state, each a complete SIHRS trajectory with its own parameters. Every week the newest admissions reweight the candidates by how well they explain the data (Liu-West jitter and systematic resampling keep the population diverse), and the surviving spread of trajectories is the forecast uncertainty. This sequential design matches the operational cadence: forecasts update weekly as data arrive, rather than refitting from scratch.

BioNetGen's BNG2.pl is invoked once per state cell when a run is prepared, expanding the BNGL model into its reaction network. During fitting, the particle filter integrates that network's differential equations with bngsim, an in-process BioNetGen simulation engine: no per-simulation BNG2.pl subprocess is launched, so a full state-season fit completes in seconds.

Research: the two-strain variant not in the shipped ensemble

This section documents a research variant. It is not part of the submitted forecast, and it has no tab in the primary navigation. It is recorded here because it passed the turning-point gate twice while failing the ensemble gate, which is a result worth keeping.

What it does. The two-strain SIHRS variant models influenza A and influenza B as independent transmission circuits, each with its own seasonally varying transmission rate and its own peak timing. Each circuit carries its own susceptible, hospitalized, and recovered pools spanning the whole population, so infection with one strain confers no protection against the other. Observed admissions are the ascertained sum of the two circuits' admission fluxes, which lets the model represent seasons in which an early A wave is followed by a late B wave.

{{ dg.sihrs2(uid='m2') }}

A second observation channel. Fitting adds a second likelihood channel to the particle filter. In addition to weekly NHSN admissions, each candidate epidemic is weighed by weekly typed positives from NREVSS clinical laboratories, treated as binomial counts of influenza A among typed specimens, so the filter learns the A/B mix as well as the admissions level. The initial A/B mix at the season start is also set from this typed surveillance series rather than fitted. Both channels use vintage data; typed counts enter only through the week preceding the forecast date, matching the NREVSS release cadence.

{{ dg.eq_pf2s() }}

The verdict. On turning-point cells the two-strain member scored relWIS 0.953 against 0.993 for the single-strain particle filter, and it cleared that turn-phase gate twice, first on the state panel and then on the full grid. On the plateau season, the season the single-strain filter handles worst, it scored 0.968 against 1.023. The gate it failed is the one that decides membership: on identical full-grid cells the equal-weight three-member ensemble scored 0.719 against 0.704 for the two-member blend. Adding it therefore makes the submitted forecast worse, so it is not part of the shipped ensemble and the blend stays at two members. Both of those numbers were measured before the 2021-22 donor exclusion, which moved the two-member reference to 0.678; the three-member blend carries the analogue at one third and so gains less from the change, but it has not been re-scored.

The engine remains available for research runs. The two-strain model page stays reachable by direct URL, and the run endpoint still accepts a three-member request, so the variant can be scored and developed further without appearing in the operational path.

The calendar analogue

{{ dg.analogue_ratios(uid='ma') }}

The analogue is a purely empirical member with no epidemiological mechanism. For a forecast at horizon h it collects donor weeks: every week from strictly earlier seasons, in any state, whose calendar week falls within two weeks of the forecast date. For each donor it records the growth ratio between admissions h weeks after the donor week and admissions in the donor week itself. The forecast distribution is the last observed value scaled by the empirical quantiles of those growth ratios; a cell with fewer than 30 usable donors produces no forecast. Donors are pooled across states because a single state supplies only about five donors per prior season inside the calendar window, far short of the 30-donor minimum, and they never come from the season being forecast. The analogue uses the archive of weekly NHSN admissions and nothing else. In the sealed record its strength lies at the middle horizons and in seasons that behave unusually, where it anchors the ensemble; the particle filter is the stronger member at the shortest horizon.

One prior season is excluded from the donor pool: 2021-22. It peaked at epiweek 16, in April 2022, while every other donor season peaked between epiweek 48 and epiweek 6, and the archived surveillance series begins 2022-02-05, so that season survives only as its growth phase. A calendar-matched pool asks what happened in March and 2021-22 answers that the epidemic was still growing: it is the only donor season whose March ratios have a median above one, with a far heavier upper tail than any other season's. Excluding it was pre-registered under hash 8f3c7a45a989e905 and improves the shipped ensemble by 3.66 percent pooled, bootstrap interval 1.83 to 6.07 percent, positive in all three sealed seasons and in 50 of 52 jurisdictions. A depth control settles the mechanism: subsampling the full pool at random to the same size moves the score by 0.20 percent, so the gain is which season is in the pool and not how many donors it holds. The effect concentrates late in the season without being confined to February: of the 15,460 sealed cells, 9,363 change, being every February, March, April and May cell together with 624 of the 2,080 January cells, which are the final January week of each season. Nothing in October, November or December changes, because that season's archived series does not begin until February 2022 and the calendar match reaches only two weeks either side of the forecast week.

The ensemble

{{ dg.ensemble_mech(uid='me') }}

The submitted forecast blends the particle filter with the calendar analogue described above by averaging their forecast quantiles. The two members fail differently; the mechanistic model can track turning points the analogue cannot anticipate, while the analogue holds the middle horizons and stays anchored when a season behaves unusually. The members carry equal, unfitted weights: 50/50 across the two shipped members, and equal thirds if a research run adds the two-strain variant, which the submitted ensemble does not. Fitting the weights was evaluated and rejected: applying the frozen per-horizon table to the three-season replay scores worse than the equal blend (pooled relWIS 0.696 against 0.678), and the leave-one-season-out fit recorded at the 2026-08-17 freeze was 0.717 against the 0.704 the equal blend scored at the time. No weight is tuned on any season.

Measured performance

Three seasons replayed in real-time conditions: each week the models saw only that week's archived data vintage, submitted, and moved on. Scores are relative weighted interval scores (relWIS) against the official FluSight baseline, ratio of sums; values below 1 beat the baseline. The convention is stated in full under Data and verification policies below.

Data and verification policies

Every run uses vintage data: the data exactly as archived on its forecast date, never revised history. Every run is seeded and reproducible from its ledger entry. Missing weeks are never imputed; they are dropped as rows with calendar-true offsets. All scoring uses the weighted interval score against the official FluSight baseline, the competition's own yardstick, and evaluation touches only data that was available at forecast time.

One measured qualification: the hub names each archived vintage file by the newest data week it contains, not by the day it was captured, and for seven of the 76 sealed as-of weeks the archived snapshot differs from what was public by that week's submission deadline (six carry later revisions or a data week the deadline feed lacked; one is stale). Season 2025-26 is clean on all 22 of its weeks. The sealed scores are also computed on the members' internal forecasts at float precision, not on the floored, rounded submission files the live console writes, a measured difference at the third decimal. Both effects are itemized in the release record's seal caveats (docs/RELEASE-1.0.md).

A scored cell is one location, one forecast week, one horizon. A cell counts only where settled truth exists and is positive, the forecast median is positive, and the FluSight baseline also covers that cell. Official FluSight scoring keeps cells this rule drops, so a count here can differ from a count published there.

Placement against the full FluSight field, meaning a rank among all submitting teams, was withdrawn on 2026-08-24 and is not restated. The reason is a difference between the two halves of that claim. A relWIS figure here is this project's own score under one stated convention, computed end to end from forecasts and scores this repository holds. A rank asserts something further, that every other team was scored on that same convention, and the field's scores come from a builder that lives outside this repository and cannot be rerun from it. The console computes the dashboard's pairwise figure for this project's own forecasts where the field's cached scores happen to be present, and it still prints no rank.

Three things went wrong when the withdrawn table was re-measured, and the field builder is only the first. The scorer that produced the archived standings does not survive. A reconstruction of the 2023-24 field from this repository's own scoring code reproduced no archived row exactly. And this project's own three rows in that field were not computed on one convention: the 2024-25 row carried the leave-one-season-out fitted ensemble weights that this project evaluated, rejected, and does not ship. The claim returns when the field and this project's entry have been scored again, end to end, on one stated convention and on the shipped configuration; until then no placement is claimed anywhere in this application. The full account is in the release record.
