Forecast the flu season, mechanistically.

FluBNF turns a compartmental disease model into weekly probabilistic forecasts for all 52 US jurisdictions — run, blended, scored, and packaged for the CDC FluSight competition, from one window.

{{ map_svg | safe }}

The weekly outlook map — each state colored by where its admissions are most likely headed. It fills in when you run a forecast.

How it works

A SIHRS model — Susceptible, Infected, Hospitalized, Recovered — written in BNGL and fitted with PyBNF's sequential particle filter on the BNGsim engine. Ten thousand candidate epidemics per state are reweighted each week by the newest data; their spread is the forecast uncertainty.

Two heads, one forecast

The mechanistic model is paired with a calendar analogue — pure historical memory. Their errors disagree in useful ways, so the blended ensemble beats either alone. Weights are frozen before the season from held-out years; nothing is tuned on the season being forecast.

Honest by construction

Every run uses the data as it looked that week, never revised history. Every run is seeded and reproducible from its ledger entry. Missing weeks stay missing. Scores are weighted interval scores against the official FluSight baseline — the competition's own yardstick.
